## Still v12, but not quiet

Mainnet ran under the [v12 / Kermit's Mom](/blocks/2022) rules all year: **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks**, **{{stat:total_superblocks}} superblocks**, **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**, **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC** moved, **{{stat:grc_minted}} GRC** minted. No planned fork happened. An unplanned one did.

## The treasury vote

On January 16, the **Treasury and Mandatory Sidestaking** poll opened on chain, after a proposal in the project's task repo ([^Treasury and Mandatory Sidestaking poll, Gridcoin-Tasks #263](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Tasks/issues/263)). The question was how Gridcoin should pay for itself. The design: a protocol-enforced sidestake out of staking rewards, sent to a treasury parent wallet and three child wallets for development, research and distributed-computing grants, with recipients set by administrative contract so the allocation can change without another fork.

When the poll closed on February 27, the winning answer was "Yes, if the maximum mandatory sidestake is 25%," ahead of a 10% option. Participation cleared the validation threshold. The 25% figure is only the ceiling written into consensus, though. The actual rate was left for a later poll, and as of October 2026 that poll hasn't been held and no mandatory recipient has been set ([^Gridcoin Research source code, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research)).

## Miss Piggy and the accidental fork

**5.4.6.0 "Miss Piggy"** came out on March 2 as a leisure release ([^5.4.6.0 release notes, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/releases/tag/5.4.6.0)). It added the CMake build system alongside autotools (Windows builds didn't work under CMake yet) and shipped the sidestake registry and editor that mandatory sidestaking would later use. A small hotfix followed two days later for a build failure on Arch Linux.

The release was missing one guard. It should have kept contract messages at version 2 until the v13 fork. Instead, 5.4.6.0 nodes started sending version 3 contracts straight away, older nodes rejected them, and on March 8 the chain split at [block 3,190,604](/block/3190604). The 5.4.6.0 side had more stake behind it and became the chain, which turned a leisure release into a mandatory one overnight. The maintainer, Jim Owens, took the blame in public: "This mistake is mine and mine alone," and the first forking incident "in a number of years."

The cleanup took two more releases. 5.4.7.0 on March 13 disconnected nodes running 5.4.5.0 and older ([^5.4.7.0 release notes, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/releases/tag/5.4.7.0)). 5.4.8.0 on April 11 added checkpoints past the fork so stragglers had to resync, routed MRC staker fees through the sidestake code and added a `listmandatorysidestakes` RPC. A poll on stress-testing consensus followed in March.

## The whitelist

Denis@Home joined the whitelist on June 12 after a poll in May and June. The University of San Jorge blocks visitors from 29 countries, which led to a second poll on whether IP-restricted projects should be eligible at all. That one ended in a near tie. Denis ran out of work and left the list again on July 31. TN-Grid, Universe@home, SRBase, ODLK1, Rosetta@home and World Community Grid all spent time on the greylist during the year.

## Exchanges

SouthXchange announced in January that it was closing, and gridcoin.us replaced it with FreiExchange. Flyp.me came off the list in June. That left SafeTrade and FreiExchange. A proposal in October put GRC's market cap at about $1.4 million, at a price of roughly a third of a cent, and noted that CoinMarketCap no longer showed GRC charts.

## The reward polls

That October proposal, Gridcoin-Tasks #268, was the start of the year's biggest decision ([^Reward rebalancing proposal, Gridcoin-Tasks #268](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Tasks/issues/268)). It argued that Gridcoin's rewards had become too small to attract anyone and proposed raising both staking and research rewards. Two polls opened on December 15: one on whether to rebalance the reward and vote-weight calculations at all, and one on how much to raise rewards, with options from no change up to 2000%. Both closed in [January 2025](/blocks/2025). The result reached mainnet in [June 2026](/blocks/2026) as a fivefold increase.

By late 2024 the active development branch was aimed almost entirely at the next mandatory release, the Natasha milestone, which had opened in February. Its first testnet builds came out in October and November.
