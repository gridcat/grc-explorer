## Fern, and the version number that matters

If [2018](/blocks/2018) changed Gridcoin's economics, **Fern** changed almost everything else. It shipped as the mandatory **5.0.0.0** release on **September 3, 2020**, and its hard fork activated at [block 2,053,000](/block/2053000) on **October 4, 2020**.

Here is the correction this archive exists to make. Fern is the **v11** fork. You will see it written, including in earlier drafts of this very page, that Fern stayed "compatible with the v10 protocol." That is wrong. Block 2,053,000 is exactly where the chain's block version steps from 10 to 11, and v11 is the largest consensus break in Gridcoin's history: contracts go binary, the superblock format changes, and the claim moves off the old `hashBoinc` string into a real coinbase contract. If you only remember one fact about Fern, make it that one: Fern is v11, October 2020, block 2,053,000.

The release announcement did not undersell it. The maintainer's own words were that this was "essentially a complete rewrite": roughly 240 pull requests over about ten months, with practically the entire Gridcoin-specific layer sitting on top of the vanilla proof-of-stake code rebuilt ([^Fern release announcement — jamescowens, hive.blog](https://hive.blog/gridcoin/@jamescowens/gridcoin-5-0-0-0-mandatory-fern-release)). Public credit went to the contributor cyrossignol, "whose incredible contributions formed the backbone of this release."

## What Fern actually did

**Reward calculation got honest.** Research rewards used to be averaged over a rolling window, which was convenient and brought ugly side effects: a first-stake cap and a six-month reward expiry. Fern computes a researcher's reward directly from the magnitudes in each superblock between their stakes. No averaging artifact, and both old constraints removed.

**Beacons got a lifecycle.** This is the long tail of the [2017 security paper](/blocks/2017). Registration now requires a one-time verification through a temporary BOINC username change, proving the wallet owner controls the BOINC account. The advertise fee jumped from a token 0.00001 GRC to 0.5 GRC, small in absolute terms but enough to make spam beacons pointless. Beacons expire after 180 days and need renewal, and crucially, research you already earned is kept even if the beacon lapses. Only future earning pauses.

**The team requirement died.** Researchers no longer had to join a specific Gridcoin BOINC team to get paid. People had wanted this gone for years, with half-serious forum threads claiming it alone capped the user base. Fern finally removed it at the v11 height without breaking magnitude attribution.

**Sync stopped being painful.** The announcement claimed a decent machine on a good connection could sync the entire mainnet chain from genesis in under four hours. Anyone who had brought up a node the hard way before Fern felt that one.

## What the year felt like on-chain

**{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks** were minted across 2020, with a discontinuity in early October where the rules changed under the chain mid-stride. **{{stat:total_superblocks}} superblocks**, **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**, **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC** moved. Block 0 is still block 0; the chain did not restart. But the rules around who a researcher is, how they get paid, and how you stand up a node became what they still are today. Follow-up leisure releases (5.0.1.0, then 5.0.2.0 on October 8) cleaned up the edges a major cut always leaves.

Everything later in this archive, manual reward claims, v12, mandatory sidestaking, the upcoming v13 and v14, is a renovation on Fern's structure. Fern itself is the reference point the rest of the chronicle measures distance from.
