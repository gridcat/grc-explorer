## The chain you are actually looking at

[Block 0](/block/0) carries the timestamp **October 11, 2014, 13:22 UTC**. Every count in this archive starts from it. The genesis block's time is written into the code, though. The first block anyone actually mined, [block 1](/block/1), came a day later, at 22:27 UTC on October 12.

Gridcoin Research was Rob Halförd's answer to a question Bitcoin never answered well. The network was burning the power budget of a small country on hashes that produced nothing but more hashes. What if the same incentive paid for protein folding, climate models or cancer screening instead? [Gridcoin Classic](/blocks/2013) had tried that in 2013 and failed. Research was the second attempt. The code had been in the works since at least August 2014, when the first commit landed in the repository.

## How it started

The chain didn't begin under proof-of-research. The first 2,050 blocks were ordinary scrypt proof-of-work, there to bootstrap the chain safely before staking switched on ([^Proof-of-Research history, Gridcoin-Wiki archive](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Wiki/wiki/zArchive~Proof-of-Research)). [Block 2050](/block/2050), on October 13, is the last of them. From there the chain ran on staking with a research reward on top, a proof-of-stake design that descends from Peercoin by way of Novacoin and Blackcoin.

Two ideas made it interesting from the first week:

- Beacons. Your identity on the network is a signed message tying a Gridcoin address to a BOINC CPID, not a mining rig. Register once, keep contributing and keep getting paid, with no hardware arms race.
- Magnitude. The network measures how much each researcher contributes to whitelisted BOINC projects, and research rewards follow from that. The official thread at launch showed World Community Grid, Rosetta@home and POEM@home as examples.

## The foundation block

On launch day, [block 10](/block/10) minted 340,569,880 GRC in one go, by far the largest block in the chain's history ([^Proof-of-Research genesis block, Gridcoin-Wiki archive](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Wiki/wiki/zArchive~Proof-of-Research-Genesis-Block)). It wasn't a premine in the usual sense. Nobody sold it and no developer got an allocation. It was the projected Classic supply times ten, created to pay out the 1-to-10 conversion. Classic holders who burned their coins got paid from it, and whatever was never claimed became the foundation fund.

## Rewards and the first fixes

The early reward schedule was generous and stepped down on fixed dates written into the code. Staking interest started at 9% a year, and the cap on research rewards per stake started at 500 GRC. The first step down came on November 30: 8% interest and a 400 GRC cap. Both kept falling through 2015.

Two fixes in December left marks that are still visible in the data:

- [Block 85400](/block/85400), on December 14, switched on ProtocolV2 and stretched the block target from 60 to 90 seconds, where it has been ever since.
- From [block 91387](/block/91387) to block 91500, starting December 19, the code forced a difficulty reset. A source comment explains why: difficulty was "sticking at 2065 due to many incompatible features."

The year ended with 3.3.2.1, a mandatory upgrade, on December 30.

## What the numbers say

2014 is a partial year, October to December: **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks** and **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**. Two other figures stand out, and they have the same cause.

**{{stat:grc_minted}} GRC minted** in under three months is more than any later year by far. Nearly all of it is block 10. **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC moved** is an order of magnitude above anything that follows, because conversions were being paid out of the foundation block and an entire coin's holders were moving across in a few months. The chain was being filled, not used yet.

You won't see any superblocks for 2014, and that's right. The daily magnitude snapshot only arrived in mid-2015. (The explorer's superblock count stays at zero well past that for a different reason, explained on the [2015 page](/blocks/2015).)

## What 2014 set up

By New Year the basics were in place: proof-of-stake security, beacons, the magnitude idea and a working link to BOINC. The open questions are the ones the rest of this archive answers. Can the network agree on a daily snapshot without a trusted operator? Can the reward economics survive whales and a changing project list? Each became the main fight of a later year.
