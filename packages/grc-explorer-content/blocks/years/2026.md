## Two forks, two days

The mandatory 5.5.0.0 release, codenamed **"Natasha,"** came out on April 3, 2026 ([^5.5.0.0 release notes, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/releases/tag/5.5.0.0)). It had been thirteen months in the making: 78 pull requests, 435 commits, eleven contributors. The release date started an eight-week countdown. The rules themselves switched on at fixed heights.

[Block 3,989,800](/block/3989800) was the first v13 block, staked at 20:25 UTC on May 31. [Block 3,990,000](/block/3990000) followed at 01:55 UTC on June 1 as the first v14 block. Nothing dramatic happened at either height. The longest gap between blocks around the two activations was a little over eleven minutes, there was no visible reorg, and the chain just kept going under new rules.

The upgrade itself had gone smoothly. Blocks staked by 5.5.0.0 wallets went from about 300 a day just after release to more than 800 by late April. The last 5.4.x stakers stopped producing blocks when the fork arrived.

## What v13 changed

People call v13 "Fern II," after the [2020 rewrite](/blocks/2020), and the name is fair. Most of it changes how the network handles money, projects and governance:

- Mandatory sidestaking: coinstakes can carry up to four protocol-enforced sidestake outputs, capped at 25% of the reward in total. This is the [2024 treasury vote](/blocks/2024) written into consensus. As of early October the registry is still empty, so no mandatory recipient has been set and every sidestake you see on chain is voluntary.
- Superblock v3 and ProjectV4 contracts: superblocks now carry per-project credit data for every CPID. Every whitelisted project was re-issued as a v4 contract within a day of activation.
- AutoGreylist: the network now greylists a BOINC project on its own when its credit activity drops (zero-credit days, a whitelist activity score), rather than waiting for a manual decision. Right after activation, seven projects got a master-signed override that keeps them active: Einstein@home, Folding@home, MilkyWay@home, NFS@home, NumberFields@home, ODLK1 and YoYo@home.
- Adjustable reward parameters: the constant block reward and the magnitude unit were hard-coded before. Now they are read from administrative protocol entries, within ceilings set in the code (500 GRC per block, 5 GRC per magnitude per day). The poll magnitude-weight factor works the same way.

## What v14 changed

v14 is the HTLC fork. It ports Bitcoin's BIP65, BIP68 and BIP112 (`OP_CHECKLOCKTIMEVERIFY`, `OP_CHECKSEQUENCEVERIFY`, sequence-locked inputs) and adds `createhtlc`, `claimhtlc` and `refundhtlc` RPCs. Those are the primitives a trustless cross-chain atomic swap needs. The release ships the building block. A swap protocol on top of it would be separate work, and Natasha doesn't include one.

v14 also introduces **v3 beacons**, which prove ownership of a BOINC account with an RSA-SHA512 signature from the project server rather than [Fern's](/blocks/2020) temporary username change. In early October, every one of the roughly 1,560 active beacons still uses the older email-verify method. The new kind exists, but nobody has registered one.

## The fivefold raise

The biggest change of the year came from governance, not from the fork itself. Two polls that closed in January 2025 had settled it in advance. "Proposed rebalancing of the reward and vote weight calculations," Gridcoin-Tasks #268, passed with a large majority ([^Reward rebalancing proposal, Gridcoin-Tasks #268](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Tasks/issues/268)). A companion poll asked how much to raise staking and research rewards, and 500% came first, with about 42% of the vote weight across seven options. In April 2025 a third poll agreed that numeric multi-choice polls are decided first-past-the-post. That made "500%" binding rather than the weighted average of about 979%.

v13 made that result possible to apply without another fork, and about nine hours after v14 the admin keys applied it. Two protocol entries in [block 3,990,094](/block/3990094) on June 1 set:

- the constant block reward from 10 GRC to 76.29373193 GRC per block;
- the magnitude unit from 0.25 to 1.0305 GRC per unit of magnitude per day.

The odd decimals come from the method. Pre-fork emission was about 38,350 GRC a day. Five times that is roughly 191,750, and the golden ratio split it: 38.2% to the block reward (73,242 GRC a day across 960 target blocks) and 61.8% to research (118,508 GRC a day across about 115,000 network magnitude). A third entry set the poll magnitude-weight factor to 0.618034, the same ratio again. That one changes voting weight, not issuance.

You can see the step in the data. [Block 3,990,094](/block/3990094) minted 24.71 GRC, and the [next block](/block/3990095) minted 76.63. Monthly issuance went from about 1.17 million GRC in January through May to between 4.6 and 5.6 million a month from June on. The chain's own money-supply counter agrees with the explorer's sums. On the trailing thirty days, research takes 61.5% of the new coins, close to the 61.8% target.

The release notes put the planned inflation at about 14% a year, up from 2.8%. The realised rate so far is closer to 12%, for two reasons. Research rewards are only minted when someone stakes or files a claim. And the chain has been producing about 876 blocks a day instead of 960. That slowdown is its own puzzle: it began in mid-May, about two weeks before the fork, when daily stakers dropped from around 470 to around 350, and it has held since. Difficulty didn't fall to match.

## The June 8 crash

AutoGreylist brought the year's one real incident. On June 8, one BOINC project's credit numbers landed on an integer-truncation edge in the whitelist activity score calculation. The code divided by zero and threw an exception that nothing caught. Every node computes the same greylist, so every scraper-subscriber node aborted at about the same moment ([^5.5.1.0 hotfix, PR #3012, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/pull/3012)). Blocks kept coming (the worst gap that morning was under fifteen minutes) and 5.5.1.0 shipped the same day with the guard. It was labelled a leisure release, but in practice it was mandatory. By mid-June 5.5.1.0 staked almost every block. A few 5.5.0.0 nodes still stake a dozen or two blocks a day in late September.

On the chain: **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks**, **{{stat:total_superblocks}} superblocks**, **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**, **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC** moved and **{{stat:grc_minted}} GRC** minted so far, about four fifths of the minting after June 1.

The project list moved more than usual. Rosetta@home dropped off the whitelist on January 30 and came back on June 1. World Community Grid was removed on April 6 and re-added on May 15. SiDock@home left in April and returned on June 1. Amicable Numbers was removed on April 8 and is still out. A March poll on adding PrimeGrid failed.

The biggest vote of the late summer, Gridcoin-Tasks #276, asked what to do with on-chain messaging. An overwhelming majority chose to remove it in its current form, citing chain bloat and the legal risk of storing arbitrary content. No mainnet release has made that change.

The price was thin all year: a low near a quarter of a cent on June 30, then a run in September to a 2026 high of about three cents on September 25, and back under a cent by October.
