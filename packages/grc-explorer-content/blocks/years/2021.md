## Fern's first full year

[Fern](/blocks/2020) was the biggest change the chain had ever taken, and 2021 was its first full year in production: **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks**, **{{stat:total_superblocks}} superblocks**, **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions** and **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC** moved, all under the new beacon lifecycle and reward calculation, with no team requirement.

Most of it simply worked. Wallets renewed beacons on their own. Removing the team requirement didn't break attribution. By the end of the year, nobody called the post-Fern protocol "the new way Gridcoin works" any more.

## A busy March

The exception was the newbie bug, which [Gladys](/blocks/2020) had only partly fixed: new beacons were still losing their accrued research at superblocks. **5.2.0.0 "Hilda,"** a mandatory release, came out on March 1 with a permanent fix and back pay for the affected researchers ([^5.2.0.0 release notes, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/releases/tag/5.2.0.0)). It also cut memory use by at least 30% and brought sync from zero under two hours. The block version stayed at 11, but it was a real consensus fork. It activated at [block 2,197,000](/block/2197000) just after midnight UTC on March 15, and nodes that hadn't upgraded were disconnected after block 2,199,000.

Two point releases came before the fork. 5.2.1.0 arrived on March 7, and 5.2.2.0 on March 14 fixed a stall that stopped fresh nodes from syncing past block 2,069,264.

Then the fork showed that Hilda had fixed 127 of the 130 affected CPIDs. Nodes disagreed about the other three. 5.3.0.0, an emergency mandatory release with no codename, shipped on March 16, the day after the fork. It made every node rebuild its accrual snapshots so they would agree again ([^5.3.0.0 release notes, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/releases/tag/5.3.0.0)). 5.3.1.0 in April added a check of hardened checkpoints at startup and fixed a beacon-renewal bug that had cost one researcher a superblock's worth of magnitude.

## Ingrid

**5.3.2.0 "Ingrid,"** on August 1, was a leisure release, but it was the one people noticed. It reskinned the wallet using the GUI design the community had funded by poll back in 2018. It also moved the code to C++17, ported Bitcoin's settings handling (with a new `gridcoinsettings.json`), added a wizard for consolidating unspent outputs and overhauled the voting screens.

## Money and the whitelist

In February, a proposal in the project's task tracker asked how to use the foundation's funds. In May, a poll on paying for past development work passed comfortably. "One wallet, one vote" was also put to a poll that spring and was voted down by a wide margin. A paper on a closed-loop economy for Gridcoin was published in May. The mandatory sidestaking that eventually [funded the project from the protocol](/blocks/2026) wasn't on the table yet. These were the first organised conversations about funding.

The whitelist kept changing:

- Minecraft@home was added after a poll in February and March.
- SiDock@home needed two polls. The first, in the spring, had near-unanimous support but didn't reach the participation threshold. The second passed in June, and the project was whitelisted at the end of the month.
- GPUGrid was greylisted on July 28 and Asteroids@home on August 6, both for lack of available work.

## On the market

GRC's 2021 high was about two cents, well up on 2020's peak of a little over one. That was enough for the community to hold a fireside chat in January about getting GRC onto more exchanges, a topic that hasn't gone away since.
