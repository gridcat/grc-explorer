## The year the reward model changed

2018 opened at the top. In the first days of January, GRC hit its all-time high, somewhere around twenty-one cents depending on which exchange and which tracker you trust ([^Gridcoin price history — CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/gridcoin-research)). It has never been back. The rest of the year is the long ride down that the whole market took, but on-chain the important thing is not the price. It is that Gridcoin used 2018 to fix the economics it had been arguing about for two years.

By the numbers it was a heavy year: **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks**, **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**, **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC** moved, **{{stat:grc_minted}} GRC** minted. And the superblock count finally looks like a real cadence rather than a trickle, because the [2017 stabilisation](/blocks/2017) held.

## Two forks, one that mattered

The chain crossed two version gates this year. [Block 1,144,000](/block/1144000) on **January 23** brought the V9 tally system, a quieter accounting change. The one people remember is [block 1,420,000](/block/1420000) on **November 6, 2018**: the **V10** hard fork, shipped in the mandatory **4.0.0.0** release codenamed "Betsy" three weeks earlier on October 19 ([^Gridcoin 4.0.0.0 release notes — github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/releases/tag/4.0.0.0)).

Before V10, a staker earned an interest-like rate that scaled with the size of their stake, layered on top of any research reward. That coupling did exactly what you would expect. It paid the largest holders the most for doing the least, and it made the network's mint rate hard to predict because it floated with how much coin was staking. After V10, every block mints a fixed amount, full stop. Predictable per block, predictable per day, and you can finally do arithmetic on the money supply.

You can see the network react. With a flat reward suddenly making staking worthwhile for everyone, more wallets came online to stake, and difficulty climbed from low single digits into the high teens within weeks. People noticed their odds change in real time.

## What else Betsy carried

A mandatory release is a chance to clear the backlog, and 4.0.0.0 used it:

- Coinstake output count raised to 8, which let Linux nodes stake superblocks through forwarded contracts where the old limit had blocked them.
- The upstream Bitcoin hash implementation replaced Gridcoin's older one.
- The legacy "neural network" code and the old mint limiter were torn out.

It did not all land cleanly. A **4.0.1.0** hotfix followed on November 30 to kill the root cause of some bad forks people hit right after the upgrade. That is a normal scar from a real hard fork, and worth recording rather than smoothing over.

## One thing this is not

It is worth being blunt about a name people conflate. **This is not Fern.** Fern is the 5.0.0.0 release and the v11 fork, and it is [two years away in 2020](/blocks/2020). The 2018 fork changed the reward formula and nothing deeper; the codebase rewrite, beacon overhaul, and team-requirement removal that people mean when they say "Fern" all come later. 4.0.0.0 is "Betsy," it is v10, it is the constant-block-reward fork, and conflating it with Fern gets the chain's history wrong by two years and one major version.

What it set up is solid ground. Every later reward design, Fern, manual reward claims, mandatory sidestaking, sits on top of the constant-block-reward floor that switched on at block 1,420,000. Nothing after this replaces it. They all build on it.
