## A predictable year

2018's [4.0.0.0 hard fork](/blocks/2018) gave the chain its first predictable mint rate. 2019 was the first full calendar year under that regime — {{stat:total_blocks}} blocks, {{stat:total_superblocks}} superblocks, {{stat:total_txs}} transactions, {{stat:grc_moved}} GRC moved between addresses — and most of the visible chain activity was simply *routine.* Stakers staked, scrapers scraped, researchers ran BOINC, magnitude payouts went out daily.

The boring stretch was the point. The 4.0.0.0 fork was meant to make the chain easier to reason about; 2019 demonstrated it had.

## What was happening off-chain

While the chain ran routinely, the development effort had quietly shifted to the next major rewrite. The community was building what would become **Fern (5.0.0.0)** — a near-complete reorganisation of the codebase touching 240+ pull requests by the time it shipped. Many of the hard architectural questions deferred during 2018 were getting their answer in 2019's design discussions and unmerged branches:

- How do we make superblock construction deterministic enough for any honest node to produce identically?
- Can the *team requirement* — researchers having to join a Gridcoin BOINC team — be removed without breaking magnitude attribution?
- Should beacons require a one-time BOINC username verification at registration, eliminating a class of identity-spoof attacks?
- Can magnitude be calculated from the actual superblocks between stakes rather than a rolling average?

None of these landed in a wallet release in 2019. They were waiting for the 5.0.0.0 cut, which would come the following year.

## Wallet line

The 4.0.x line continued through 2019 with leisure releases addressing edge cases and minor wallet improvements — none of them mandatory. The chain's protocol semantics stayed exactly as they were post-4.0.0.0 activation.
