## A quiet year on purpose

2019 is the first full calendar year the chain ran under the [constant block reward](/blocks/2018), and the consensus log has nothing in it. No version gate, no fork. **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks**, **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**, **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC** moved, **{{stat:grc_minted}} GRC** minted, and {{stat:total_superblocks}} superblocks, close to one a day for the whole year.

In 2017 the daily snapshot had once gone 186 hours without landing. In 2019 it arrived nearly every day without anyone having to watch it. Stakers staked, researchers ran BOINC and payouts went out. The 2018 fork was supposed to make the chain predictable, and 2019 showed it by being uneventful.

Every release this year was a leisure release, so none of them changed consensus. They still changed a lot.

## The releases

**4.0.2.0 "Camilla"** on April 3 brought **sidestaking** and stake splitting ([^Gridcoin Research CHANGELOG, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)). A staker could now send a fixed share of every reward to another address, a charity, a developer or a second wallet of their own. The voluntary version is from 2019. The mandatory, consensus-enforced version took until [2026](/blocks/2026).

**4.0.3.0 "Denise"** on May 10 replaced the old .NET "neural network" with a C++ scraper. The neural network was the stats system behind the [2017 superblock crisis](/blocks/2017), and the developers rewrote it out of existence instead of patching it again. Jim Owens, Tomáš Brada and Marco Nilsson put more than 250 hours into it, starting from a prototype by Paul Jensen ([^Scraper progress update, jamescowens, steemit.com](https://steemit.com/gridcoin/@jamescowens/progress-update-on-the-new-gridcoin-boinc-stats-collection-system-the-scraper-which-replaces-the-old-neural-network)).

**4.0.5.0 "Elizabeth"** on August 20 shipped Scraper 2.0 and a team whitelist. The whitelist let teams other than "Gridcoin" count, which was the first step toward getting rid of the team requirement altogether. Volunteers began testing that in August, first on testnet and then with twenty volunteer teams on mainnet in batches of five.

**4.0.6.0 "Ernestine"** on October 22 prepared the superblock format for Fern, added multisig tools and removed the legacy staking kernel.

The codenames run alphabetically, and the people who pick them seem to have a soft spot for the Muppets: Annie Sue, Camilla, Gladys and Hilda all come from that cast, and later releases are called Kermit's Mom and Miss Piggy. Nobody has stated a theme officially. Not every release gets one either. Codenames mark milestones, and the hotfixes in between go without.

## Governance gets rules

In May an on-chain poll approved formal definitions for poll types and the thresholds a poll must meet to count. The first polls run under the new rules removed Drug Discovery and Citizen Science Grid from the whitelist. Enigma@Home was dropped on May 31 because it had closed signups.

The project also took its case to the scientists. Two Gridcoin presentations ran at the BOINC Workshop in Chicago in July, and a poll later that month reimbursed the presenters. An updated white paper was approved by poll in August.

## Bittrex leaves

In July, Bittrex flagged GRC for removal. The markets went offline that month, and the wallet closed on October 4. Poloniex had already gone in [2018](/blocks/2018), so by the end of 2019 Gridcoin had lost both of its big US exchanges. GRC trades on smaller venues ever since.

## What was being built

A September roadmap set out the next phase: Fern would remove the team requirement, greylisting would be automated, and manual reward claims were "in early architecture development." The Fern milestone had been open on GitHub since May, and its main development push started late in the year ([^Fern release announcement, jamescowens, hive.blog](https://hive.blog/gridcoin/@jamescowens/gridcoin-5-0-0-0-mandatory-fern-release)). It reached mainnet in [2020](/blocks/2020).
