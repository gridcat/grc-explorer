## A year off from forks

[Kermit's Mom](/blocks/2022) had been a big mandatory release, and 2023 was the year it had to just work. The mainnet consensus log stays empty: **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks**, **{{stat:total_superblocks}} superblocks**, **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**, **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC** moved, **{{stat:grc_minted}} GRC** minted, under the same rules as the year before. Every release was a leisure release.

## Folding@home

The year opened with a first. A poll on adding Folding@home closed on January 14 with a large majority in favour, and the project went onto the whitelist the same day. Folding@home isn't a BOINC project, so it can't report credit the way the scrapers expect. An external adapter translates its statistics into a form the scrapers can read. The question had first been put to the community in a [2020 opinion poll](/blocks/2020). A second poll that closed in January set new participation thresholds for polls, and the next release enforced them.

## LaVerne and the April fixes

**5.4.2.0 "LaVerne"** on March 26 brought a hierarchical-deterministic wallet ([^5.4.2.0 release notes, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/releases/tag/5.4.2.0)). It follows BIP32, so keys come from a single seed, but it doesn't support BIP39 seed phrases. An upgraded wallet becomes a hybrid that keeps its old keys alongside the new seed. LaVerne also bundled Berkeley DB 5.3 and migrated every wallet file to it, so a wallet file can now move between operating systems. It also sped up poll handling on slow disks.

The switch to libsecp256k1 the year before turned out to have sharp edges, and three releases in April cleaned them up. 5.4.3.0 fixed the Windows installer and wallet backups. 5.4.4.0 fixed `dumpprivkey` and `importprivkey`, which had been broken since 5.4.0.0. 5.4.5.0, two days later, fixed parsing of old keys generated under OpenSSL ([^Gridcoin Research releases, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/releases)).

## Exchanges

Txbit, one of GRC's few remaining venues, stopped trading on August 14 and closed for good on September 14. SafeTrade listed GRC against BTC and USDT on August 22, and gridcoin.us swapped one for the other two days later.

## A full year of MRC

[Manual Reward Claims](/blocks/2022) had their first full year. Solo researchers used them, so claim transactions became a regular sight. Every claim sent most of its fee to the foundation and a smaller share to the staker who included it. Researchers no longer needed a wallet online to earn, because a claim could be filed weeks after the work was done.

The whitelist was busy in a quieter way. Universe@home, TN-Grid, GPUGrid and World Community Grid all went onto the greylist and came back at least once during the year, mostly over work availability. GPUGrid did it three times.

## What was being assembled

The next big upgrade took shape in design discussions. The Miss Piggy milestone opened in February, and in October a pull request brought a new sidestake registry and editor that would later carry mandatory sidestakes. A community framework for a treasury funded from staking rewards was argued through the year. It went to a formal vote in [January 2024](/blocks/2024) and became consensus with the [2026 Natasha fork](/blocks/2026).
