## The year the reward model changed

2018 started at the top. On January 8, GRC hit its all-time high of about $0.214 ([^Gridcoin price history, CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/gridcoin-research)). It has never come close since. The rest of the year was the same long slide the whole market took. On chain, the price matters less than the fact that Gridcoin used 2018 to fix the economics it had been arguing about since 2016.

It was a heavy year by the numbers: **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks**, **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**, **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC** moved, **{{stat:grc_minted}} GRC** minted. The {{stat:total_superblocks}} superblocks are still short of one a day, and the neural network that built them had a bad habit of [stalling](/blocks/2017).

## A rough winter

The year began with a mandatory release. 3.7.0.0 came out on January 8, tied to the v9 block version that started at [block 1,144,000](/block/1144000) on January 23 ([^Gridcoin Research CHANGELOG, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)). V9 changed how the network tallies research, and the transition was not clean. Through January and February nodes kept forking off and stalling on sync, exchanges put their wallets into maintenance, and five hotfix releases followed in the first three weeks. A rewrite of the reorganisation code landed in February, and a second mandatory release, 3.7.8.0, closed out the research-age and beacon bugs behind the forks on March 1.

## The community picks a reward

While the developers fought forks, the community voted on what Gridcoin should become. A set of roadmap polls closed in early February. "Solving the stake weight problem" went 90.8% to a constant block reward. A question on how to pay researchers went 55.5% to "focus on manual reward claims," a design that took four more years to ship.

The number itself came from a second poll, open from April 11 to June 6. The option "75/25 with a 10 GRC block reward" won by a wide margin. That 10 GRC is still the default in the code today.

Governance got more formal in other places too. A poll in February approved a whitelist-and-greylist process. It gave the network a way to pause rewards for a BOINC project that was misbehaving or out of work, without removing it from the list. DrugDiscovery@Home and Universe@home were the first two projects greylisted. Universe came back in March once its servers were patched for Meltdown and Spectre. Sourcefinder was voted off the whitelist that month, and ODLK1 and DHEP were added during the year.

## Two forks, one that mattered

[Block 1,420,000](/block/1420000) on November 6, 2018 is the date to remember. That is the **V10** hard fork, shipped in the mandatory 4.0.0.0 release codenamed "Betsy," which came out on October 19 ([^Gridcoin 4.0.0.0 release notes, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/releases/tag/4.0.0.0)).

Before V10, stakers earned an interest-like rate that grew with the size of their stake, on top of any research reward. It did what you would expect. The largest holders got paid the most for doing the least, and the mint rate floated with how much coin happened to be staking, so nobody could predict it. After V10, every block mints a flat 10 GRC plus whatever research reward it claims. You can do arithmetic on the money supply again.

The network noticed. With a flat reward, staking was worth it for small wallets too, more of them came online, and average difficulty roughly tripled in the weeks after the fork.

## What else Betsy carried

Mandatory releases are when the backlog gets cleared, and 4.0.0.0 cleared a lot:

- Superblock contract forwarding, which let Linux nodes stake superblocks at all.
- A higher limit on coinstake outputs, raised to eight.
- Bitcoin's hash implementation in place of Gridcoin's older one.
- Removal of obsolete neural-network code and the old mint limiter. The neural network itself, the .NET stats system behind the [2017 superblock crisis](/blocks/2017), held on until 2019.

It didn't all land cleanly. A 4.0.1.0 hotfix on November 30 fixed a wrong research-age scan range that had nodes disagreeing on rewards and forking off.

## Off-chain, and off the big exchanges

Two smaller stories from the middle of the year matter later. On August 31, the foundation's 32.4 million GRC moved from an address only Rob Halförd controlled into a 2-of-3 multisig held by three community members. And on September 25, Poloniex stopped trading GRC as part of a sweep of small coins. Bittrex followed in 2019.

The wallet also changed looks. 3.7.12.0 in May moved the build to autotools and shipped a purple GUI redesign. 3.7.14.0 in July was the first release with a codename, "Annie Sue." The codenames have run in alphabetical order ever since.

## One thing 2018 is not

Fern is the 5.0.0.0 release and the v11 fork, two years later in [2020](/blocks/2020). The 2018 fork changed the reward formula and nothing deeper. The codebase rewrite, the beacon overhaul and the end of the team requirement all came with Fern. 4.0.0.0 is "Betsy," it is v10, and it is the constant-block-reward fork.

Every later reward design builds on it. Fern, manual reward claims, mandatory sidestaking and the [2026 raise to 76 GRC per block](/blocks/2026) all adjust the flat per-block reward that switched on at block 1,420,000.
