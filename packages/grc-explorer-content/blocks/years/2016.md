## A year with no fork

Some years on this chain are inflection points. 2016 was not. **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks**, **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**, **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC** moved, **{{stat:grc_minted}} GRC** minted, and no consensus change worth a landmark. The chain just kept producing blocks every ninety seconds while the interesting things happened off-chain.

That is worth saying plainly because it is the actual texture of most of a blockchain's life. Reading this archive top to bottom you would think Gridcoin is a sequence of dramatic forks. It is mostly years like 2016, where the protocol does not move and the project argues with itself about what it is.

## What the community was arguing about

The recurring fight was distribution. By 2016 community analysts were circulating numbers like "the top 50 addresses hold roughly half of all GRC," and that landed hard on a coin whose pitch was egalitarian: anyone with a CPU can earn by doing science. A whale running industrial BOINC hardware earned far more than a hobbyist contributing something meaningful, and the reward curve did little to flatten that. None of this produced code in 2016. It produced threads, and those threads are the direct ancestors of the economic reform that finally shipped in [2018](/blocks/2018).

The other slow shift was where the community lived. Through 2016 the center of gravity moved onto Steemit. That is partly why so much of Gridcoin's primary-source history, including most release announcements, sits on Steemit and later Hive rather than on a forum or a mailing list.

## The flaw nobody had announced yet

In September 2016 a group of security researchers at Ruhr-University Bochum quietly disclosed serious vulnerabilities to the developers ([^"Breaking and Fixing Gridcoin" — USENIX WOOT'17](https://www.usenix.org/conference/woot17/workshop-program/presentation/grothe)). The public did not know. The attacks let you replay a block's research claim to steal the reward, or hijack a researcher's beacon key-pair and lock the rightful owner out of their own BOINC earnings ([^Gridcoin — Wikipedia, citing USENIX WOOT'17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gridcoin)). The dev got a reply out the next day, but a clean fix was harder than a quick reply, and this story does not resolve in 2016. It resolves, messily, [in 2017](/blocks/2017), on a conference stage.

## Superblocks: still zero, still honest

For the third year running this explorer indexes **zero superblocks**. The same caveat as 2014 and 2015 applies, and by now it is a pattern rather than a coincidence: the magnitude-snapshot system existed but did not reliably commit to the chain. The fix was a year away.

2016 ended exactly as it began: the chain on stable footing, a pile of unsolved fairness and security problems on the table, and a community that had started writing down what it wanted to do about them. All of it came due in 2017.
