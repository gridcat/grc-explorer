## The first full year

2014 was three months of launch traffic. 2015 was the first time the chain had to run for a calendar year and behave like infrastructure: **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks** and **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**, with **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC** moving between addresses. Compare the mint figure to 2014's and the shape of the year is obvious. **{{stat:grc_minted}} GRC minted**, down by more than 90% from the launch-and-conversion flood, because the chain was being lived in now, not filled up.

Two threads ran through the year.

## The reward curve gets bent down

Early Gridcoin paid generously, and the community knew it could not last. Through 2015 the subsidy was deliberately walked down. The "magnitude multiplier" was deprecated in July. By November the maximum block subsidy was capped at 50 GRC with no expiry date, and the staking interest rate had fallen from around 9% toward 1.5% over the chain's first year ([^Scraper / magnitude summary — gridcoin.us](https://gridcoin.us/assets/docs/scraper-summary.pdf)). None of this was dramatic on any single day. Added up, it was the project quietly admitting that the launch economics were a starting gun, not a steady state, and acting on it before the problem compounded.

## Research-age switches on

The structural event of the year is at [block 364501](/block/364501) on **October 22, 2015**: research-age activation, the model the community shorthand calls DPOR. Before it, research subsidies used the old, looser legacy math. After it, a researcher's reward accrues against the time and magnitude they actually accumulated between stakes. This is not a footnote. The accrual logic that switched on here is, in its bones, still how the chain pays scientists eleven years later ([^Scraper / magnitude summary — gridcoin.us](https://gridcoin.us/assets/docs/scraper-summary.pdf)). Every later reward upgrade in this archive is a renovation of this idea, not a replacement of it.

## The Classic chapter closes for good

On **April 26, 2015**, the [Classic-to-Research conversion window](/blocks/2013) shut after roughly six months. Whatever had not been moved across, plus accrued interest, rolled into the foundation fund. The two-chain era was over. From here there is one Gridcoin, and it is this one.

## Still no superblocks, and that is the honest answer

As in 2014, this explorer indexes **zero superblocks for 2015**. The magnitude-snapshot mechanism existed in concept, but a reliable daily superblock landing on-chain was not yet a thing the network could be counted on to produce. That fragility did not get genuinely fixed until 2017, and when it broke badly enough to be unignorable, [it broke in public](/blocks/2017).

What 2015 proved was narrower but real: the chain could run a full year, the economics could be steered while it ran, and the research-age foundation everything later builds on was now load-bearing.
