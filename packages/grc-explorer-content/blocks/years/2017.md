## Where the superblock count starts

Look at the superblock column on the [history index](/history). It reads zero for 2014, 2015 and 2016, and then 2017 has {{stat:total_superblocks}}. Superblocks didn't start in 2017. They had been landing since [mid-2015](/blocks/2015). The jump marks [block 1,034,700](/block/1034700), the "grandfather" height set on September 28. Below it, the wallet skips most claim and superblock validation and treats the early chain as settled. Above it, everything is checked in full, and only then does the wallet mark a block as a superblock. The first one this explorer counts is block 1,034,768. The grandfather height had been raised several times before, and this value stuck.

The rest of the chain looked routine: **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks**, **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**, **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC** moved, **{{stat:grc_minted}} GRC** minted. The drama was over whether researchers got paid at all.

## The superblock crisis

In 2017 the daily snapshot broke in public. From late June, superblocks started arriving days late. One wait ran about 112 hours. The worst ended at superblock 977511 on July 27, after 186 hours, nearly eight days without research payouts. A third gap of about 177 hours followed in September.

The cause was a pile-up. A wave of new users joined, the set of CPIDs changed between the moments different nodes downloaded their statistics, and the nodes ended up hashing different snapshots and couldn't agree. Only Windows wallets could take part in the neural network's quorum, which made the pool of voters even smaller. The community put up a 7,000 GRC bounty for help. Leiden Classical was voted off the whitelist in the same weeks.

## A new stake engine

The changelog in the repository opens in May with 3.5.8.9, which also brought voting to Linux and macOS ([^Gridcoin Research CHANGELOG, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)). The big change came with 3.6.0.1 on August 22. It set the **V8 stake engine** to activate at [block 1,010,000](/block/1010000), and on September 1 it did.

V8 rewrote the staking kernel and took magnitude out of stake weight, so being a researcher no longer made you a better staker. Some researchers were openly unhappy about losing that edge. It also fixed a set of security holes, including a way to inject a fake superblock and weaknesses in the neural network's voting. It didn't fix superblock production itself. That came in pieces: 3.6.1.0 on September 11 fixed a rounding difference that had stopped nodes from forging superblocks, and 3.6.2.0 on September 28 fixed neural-network sync. The same release closed the email leak described below and set the grandfather height.

## The paper

The Ruhr-University Bochum researchers who had reported flaws [in 2016](/blocks/2016) kept waiting. In March, the mandatory 3.5.8.7 shipped a partial fix: beacons older than six months could now be overwritten. The researchers found that this rule opened a new attack, re-registering an expired CPID's beacon and locking its owner out. Their emails in May went unanswered.

So on August 14 and 15 they presented "Breaking and Fixing Gridcoin" at USENIX WOOT'17 in Vancouver ([^"Breaking and Fixing Gridcoin," USENIX WOOT'17](https://www.usenix.org/conference/woot17/workshop-program/presentation/grothe)). Being publicly broken at a conference during the best bull run in the project's history made for a bad week. But the beacon hardening it forced is part of why the system became trustworthy later.

## The founder steps back

In mid-August the researchers' blog noted "a change in responsibility" inside the dev team. 3.6.3.0, tagged on October 9, carries the last commit under the founder's original account, which goes back to the first commit in August 2014. From 2018 the releases came from the community developers. In November, Rob Halförd floated a "Gridcoin III" proposal built on masternodes, but nothing came of it on this chain.

## Price and exchanges

The market went vertical, and GRC went with it, from about $0.007 on January 1 to about $0.14 on December 31, with the all-time high still to come in the first week of [2018](/blocks/2018). Exchanges were less steady. Bittrex warned in January that GRC risked delisting if its volume stayed low (it stayed, for now). Poloniex re-enabled GRC deposits and withdrawals on August 31, then disabled them again on September 26 after forks on the 23rd and 26th.

## What 2017 set up

Behind all of this ran an argument about who the rewards went to. A July analysis found the top 50 addresses held 48.8% of all GRC, and stake-based interest paid the biggest of them the most. The fix the community was drafting: drop the interest rate and pay stakers a flat per-block subsidy instead. It didn't ship in 2017. It shipped a year later, at block 1,420,000, in [2018](/blocks/2018).
