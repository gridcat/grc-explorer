## Natasha — the second great rewrite

After [Fern (2020)](/blocks/2020) restructured the codebase and the post-Fern years (2021-2025) accumulated incremental improvements, **Natasha (5.5.0.0)** activated on **April 3, 2026** as the next major consensus upgrade. It's the biggest mainnet event since Fern.

What Natasha brought to mainnet, all at once:

### Mandatory sidestaking (block v14)

Every coinstake transaction is now required to route a portion of the block reward — up to 25% total, across as many as four mandatory sidestake outputs — to recipients defined by an administrative contract. The Gridcoin foundation is the initial recipient; the administrative-contract design lets future allocations adapt without further hard forks. This is how the chain finally funds its own development and infrastructure from network economics rather than from voluntary donation flow.

### v3 beacons

Researcher beacons gained **cryptographic ownership proof**. Beyond the BOINC-username-change ritual that Fern introduced, v3 beacons now require the registrant to prove they control the CPID directly. The remaining identity-spoof class — registering a beacon for someone else's BOINC account by waiting for a coincidental username-change window — is closed.

### HTLC (Hash Time-Locked Contracts)

BIP65/BIP112 semantics — `OP_CHECKLOCKTIMEVERIFY` and `OP_CHECKSEQUENCEVERIFY` — ported into Gridcoin. By itself this changes nothing visible to most users. As a building block, it unlocks **atomic swaps**: trustless cross-chain trades where Gridcoin can be swapped for BTC or stablecoins without an intermediary. Atomic-swap protocol design was actively in progress by core developers during the same window; HTLC presence is the on-chain prerequisite.

### Build modernisation

The 2025 testnet line had quietly modernised the build system. Natasha shipped that to mainnet:

- **CMake** replaced the legacy autotools build.
- **Qt 6** compatibility for the GUI wallet (vs Qt 5).
- Wider compiler/distro coverage in CI (GCC 15, Boost 1.89+, Alpine Linux).

## What's next

Atomic swaps remain the most interesting downstream of HTLC — once a working swap protocol publishes, Gridcoin becomes interoperable with Bitcoin and other HTLC-supporting chains in a way it never has been before. That's a 2026-2027 unfolding, not something Natasha itself delivers.

For now, Natasha is the inflection point that made it possible.
