## Why Gridcoin Research

Bitcoin and its descendants spent the early 2010s consuming enough electricity to power small countries, entirely on hash functions whose only output was more hashes. Rob Hälford watched this and asked the obvious question: what if the compute did something useful?

Gridcoin Research, the chain you are browsing now, launched in October 2014 as his answer. Rather than pure proof-of-work, it tied block rewards to **proof-of-research** — verifiable contribution to the [Berkeley Open Infrastructure for Network Computing](https://boinc.berkeley.edu/) (BOINC). Researchers run BOINC clients on whitelisted projects (protein folding, climate modelling, cancer drug discovery, SETI@home), each project reports per-user credit back to the network, and the chain's reward function pays out proportionally to recent compute.

The thesis: cryptocurrency's incentive structure could fund science instead of carbon. {{stat:total_blocks}} blocks would be minted under that thesis in 2014.

## How the chain came alive

The [genesis block](/block/0) landed in October 2014, with the magnitude system bootstrapped from BOINC's existing credit reporting. The codebase descended from the Bitcoin/Litecoin lineage — familiar to crypto reviewers — with the proof-of-research overlay added on top of standard staking.

Two architectural ideas made the chain interesting from day one:

- **Beacons.** Rather than mining hardware, the unit of identity is a *beacon*: a signed message linking a Gridcoin wallet address to a BOINC CPID. Researchers register a beacon once; the chain remembers them and pays based on continuing contribution.
- **Superblocks.** Roughly daily, the network's *scrapers* agree on a snapshot of every whitelisted BOINC project and every researcher's recent contribution. The winning staker embeds the snapshot in a regular block — that block becomes a *superblock*, the on-chain truth source for the next ~24 hours of magnitude payouts. {{stat:total_superblocks}} superblocks were issued during 2014.

By year-end the chain had recorded {{stat:total_blocks}} blocks and processed {{stat:total_txs}} transactions. {{stat:grc_moved}} GRC moved between addresses, and {{stat:grc_minted}} GRC was minted as block rewards — the first year of the experiment.

## What 2014 set up

The questions the rest of the chronicle will work through:

- Could the *scraper quorum* function without trusted operators?
- Could *project whitelisting* keep up with the BOINC ecosystem — projects coming, going, occasionally hijacked?
- Could the *beacon system* scale to thousands of researchers without becoming a registration bottleneck?

Each became a defining problem of a later year. But the foundation — proof-of-research, beacons, superblocks — was set in 2014.
