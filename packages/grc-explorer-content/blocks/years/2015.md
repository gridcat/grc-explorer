## The first full year

2014 was three months of launch traffic. 2015 was the first time the chain had to run for a whole calendar year: **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks** and **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**, with **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC** moving between addresses. The mint figure shows the change. **{{stat:grc_minted}} GRC minted**, down more than 90% from the launch year, because there was no foundation block this time, only ordinary staking and research rewards.

The first mandatory upgrade of the year, 3.3.8.3 on January 26, took the CPID out of the staking kernel. Two weeks earlier the code had also gained a permanent cap on difficulty after another bout of runaway readings.

## One coin, on one exchange

On April 25, Poloniex opened a GRC/BTC market for Research and converted every GRCX balance it held. A day later, on April 26, the [Classic conversion window](/blocks/2013) closed after about six months. Whatever had never been claimed stayed in the foundation fund ([^Gridcoin foundation fund, gridcoin.us wiki](https://gridcoin.us/wiki/foundation.html)). From here on there is one Gridcoin, and it's this one.

## The neural network and the first superblocks

The daily magnitude snapshot, the superblock, arrived in the middle of the year. The system that built it was called the "neural network," although it wasn't one in the machine-learning sense: Windows wallets downloaded BOINC statistics, hashed them, and voted until enough of them agreed on the same snapshot. The commits behind it appeared in June, and 3.4.1.4 made it mandatory on July 3. From [block 271625](/block/271625), on July 11, the client stored verified magnitudes and marked superblocks in its block index. Superblocks came every 12 hours at first, and daily after research-age.

You won't find these superblocks in this explorer's counts, and the reason is technical. The modern wallet "grandfathers" old history: below a fixed height (block 1,034,700, from late 2017) it skips superblock validation entirely and never marks those blocks as superblocks. This explorer reads the chain through that wallet, so its superblock tally begins in late 2017. The superblocks of 2015 to 2017 are real. They just sit below the line the current software checks.

## The reward curve comes down

Early Gridcoin paid generously, and the code already had a plan for that. Rewards stepped down on dates written into it ([^Gridcoin Research source code, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research)):

- Staking interest, which started at 9% at launch, fell a point at a time through the spring (7% at the end of January, 5% by April, 4% in May, 3% in August) and landed at 1.5% on November 21, "forever" as the code comment put it. A community survey had set the start and end rates.
- The cap on research rewards per stake fell from 400 GRC to 300, then 250, 200, 150, 100 and 75, before settling at **50 GRC on November 20**.
- The "magnitude multiplier" was deprecated in July.

No single step was dramatic. Taken together, they turned the launch economics into something the chain could live on for years.

## Research-age switches on

The structural event of the year is [block 364501](/block/364501) on October 22, 2015: research-age activation, which the community calls DPOR ([^Gridcoin Research chain parameters, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/blob/master/src/chainparams.cpp)). Three mandatory releases rolled it out in three days, 3.5.1.7 to 3.5.1.9. Before it, research rewards used looser legacy math. After it, a researcher's reward accrues against the time and magnitude they actually built up between stakes.

The accrual logic that switched on here is, at heart, still how the chain pays researchers. Every later reward change in this archive, Fern's included, reworks this idea rather than replacing it.

## What 2015 proved

The chain could run a full year, its economics could be steered while it ran, and a decentralised daily snapshot could work, most of the time. "Most of the time" turned into the problem of the next two years, and it came to a head [in 2017](/blocks/2017).
