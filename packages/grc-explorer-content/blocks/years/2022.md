## The year solo crunchers got paid properly

The mandatory **5.4.0.0** release, codenamed **"Kermit's Mom,"** shipped on **August 1, 2022**, and its v12 hard fork activated at [block 2,671,700](/block/2671700) on **August 28, 2022** ([^5.4.0.0 release notes — github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/releases/tag/5.4.0.0)). The announcement had told people to expect it "on or about August 31," so it arrived a few days early; the chain's own timestamp on block 2,671,700 is the authoritative date and this archive uses it.

The headline feature is **Manual Reward Claims (MRC)**. Here is the problem it solved. If you ran BOINC on one machine and did not keep a wallet online staking, you built up research-magnitude you could barely realise. Under the constant-block-reward model the effective floor to cash out by staking was thousands of GRC of accrued value sitting idle. After Kermit's Mom, any researcher with an active beacon could compose a claim transaction for their own accrued reward at intervals as short as 14 days, starting from amounts around a single GRC, and pay a fee to whichever staker mined it ([^Kermit's Mom announcement — jamescowens, hive.blog](https://hive.blog/gridcoin/@jamescowens/gridcoin-5-4-0-0-mandatory-kermit-s-mom-release)).

That quietly rebalanced who Gridcoin is for. The reward system stopped privileging wallets that happened to also be staking nodes. A laptop running BOINC overnight could turn into GRC. Stakers got a small fee stream for including claims, so the two participant classes now had a real economic relationship rather than the crunchers being second-class.

## What else came with v12

A version gate is never one feature. v12 also tightened block-timestamp drift, brought the v3 poll contract format with enforced poll validation, refined the constant block reward, and continued the codebase modernisation Fern had set in motion ([^5.4.0.0 release notes — github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/releases/tag/5.4.0.0)). It was mandatory, so the network upgraded on schedule.

## About the name

Yes, the mandatory fork that unlocked manual reward claims is officially called "Kermit's Mom." Gridcoin's release-naming runs mostly through a long list of women's first names, with the odd Muppets cameo dropped in; the surrounding development milestone was "Miss Piggy." It is deliberate deadpan, and it is consistent enough across a decade of changelogs that it counts as institutional culture rather than a one-off joke. A project that names its consensus-critical hard fork after Kermit the Frog's mother is telling you something honest about who runs it.
