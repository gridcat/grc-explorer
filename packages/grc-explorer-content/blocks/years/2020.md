## Fern, and the version number that matters

If [2018](/blocks/2018) changed Gridcoin's economics, **Fern** changed nearly everything else. It shipped as the mandatory 5.0.0.0 release on September 3, 2020, and its hard fork activated at [block 2,053,000](/block/2053000) at 16:34 UTC on October 4, 2020.

People often get the version number wrong. Fern is the **v11** fork. You will see claims that Fern stayed "compatible with the v10 protocol," and they're mistaken. Block 2,052,999 is version 10 and block 2,053,000 is version 11. v11 is the largest consensus break in Gridcoin's history: contracts became binary, the superblock format changed, and the research claim moved out of the old `hashBoinc` text field into a proper coinbase contract.

The release announcement didn't undersell it. The maintainer, Jim Owens, called it "essentially a complete rewrite": 240 pull requests merged over more than ten months, with nearly the whole Gridcoin-specific layer on top of the proof-of-stake base rebuilt ([^Fern release announcement, jamescowens, hive.blog](https://hive.blog/gridcoin/@jamescowens/gridcoin-5-0-0-0-mandatory-fern-release)). Public credit went to the contributor cyrossignol, "whose incredible contributions formed the backbone of this release." The rewrite had started with the scraper that replaced the neural network in [2019](/blocks/2019).

## What Fern did

Reward calculation got exact. Research rewards used to be averaged over a rolling window. That was convenient but had ugly side effects: a 500 GRC cap on a CPID's first stake, a rolling five-day payment limit, and unclaimed rewards that expired after six months. Fern computes a researcher's reward directly from the magnitudes in each superblock between their stakes, and all three limits went away.

Beacons got a lifecycle. This is the long tail of the [2017 security paper](/blocks/2017). Registering now meant proving you control the BOINC account, through a one-time verification code you put in your BOINC username for up to three days. The fee to advertise a beacon went from a token 0.00001 GRC to 0.5 GRC, which is small in absolute terms but enough to make spam beacons pointless. Beacons expire after 180 days and the wallet renews them automatically from day 150. If a beacon lapses, research you already earned is kept, and only future earning pauses. Every existing beacon had to be re-advertised and verified within 14 days of the fork, so mid-October was busy.

The team requirement went away. Researchers no longer had to join Team Gridcoin on BOINC to get paid. The requirement dated back to [Classic](/blocks/2013), people had wanted it gone for years, and the 2019 team-whitelist trials had prepared for removing it. Fern dropped it at the v11 height without breaking magnitude attribution.

Sync got fast. The announcement said a fast machine could sync the whole mainnet chain from genesis in under four hours, about 2.5 on good hardware. Anyone who had brought up a node before Fern felt that one.

Fern also rebuilt the voting system, reimplemented transaction messages as contracts, introduced new fee rules for v11 transactions and contracts, and added a beacon wizard for new users.

## Gladys, two months later

A rewrite this size leaves bugs, and one mattered. Newly confirmed beacons had their accrued research reset to zero at each superblock, the "newbie bug." **5.1.0.0 "Gladys,"** another mandatory release, shipped on November 1 with a fix and a second hard fork at [block 2,104,000](/block/2104000), reached on December 1 ([^5.1.0.0 release notes, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/releases/tag/5.1.0.0)). It also cut the maximum poll duration to 90 days. The in-wallet upgrade notice mislabelled it as a leisure release, which didn't help. (The newbie bug wasn't completely gone either. The rest of the story is on the [2021 page](/blocks/2021).)

## The BOINC world in 2020

SETI@home, a BOINC mainstay since 1999, stopped handing out new work on March 31 and went into hibernation. Two on-chain polls asked how to respond during the COVID-19 pandemic: a social fund for SETI@home and extra rewards for "critical" projects. Both failed. In November, cyrossignol asked in an opinion poll whether Gridcoin should reward Folding@home, which isn't a BOINC project at all. 94.2% said yes. An adapter made that possible, and Folding@home joined the whitelist [in January 2023](/blocks/2023).
