## The year economic reform got serious

By 2017 the open problems with Gridcoin's original reward economics were no longer abstract: three years of on-chain data showed exactly which incentives worked and which produced perverse outcomes. The community-built reform that would land as **4.0.0.0** in November 2018 was designed, debated, and prototyped through this year.

The big open problem: **interest-based block reward**. The original chain paid stakers an interest-like rate that scaled with stake size, on top of research rewards. Combined with magnitude payouts, this produced compounding effects whales could exploit and the network could not predictably budget around.

Three threads ran in parallel:

- **Constant block reward.** Detaching the staker portion from interest and pinning it to a flat per-block subsidy. This is what would actually ship in 2018's 4.0.0.0.
- **Coinstake output expansion.** Letting a single coinstake transaction carry more outputs (eventually 8) so superblocks could be staked even on Linux nodes that previously hit limits.
- **Cryptographic modernisation.** Porting the upstream Bitcoin hash implementation to keep the codebase aligned with mainline crypto-protocol best practice.

None of these landed in 2017's wallet releases — they were design work, not yet code. But every 4.0.0.0 feature traces back to a 2017 thread.

## What 2018 would change

The 4.0.0.0 mandatory release activated at **block 1,420,000** on November 8, 2018 — replacing the interest-based reward system with a flat constant block reward. It was the chain's first major economic-policy hard fork. (The bigger architectural rewrite, codenamed **Fern**, would come two years later as 5.0.0.0 — see [2020](/blocks/2020).)
