## The first full year

2014 was launch. 2015 was the year proof-of-research had to actually work, every day, across {{stat:total_blocks}} blocks and {{stat:total_superblocks}} superblocks.

The architecture set up in [2014](/blocks/2014) — beacons, superblocks, scraper quorum — had been sufficient to start a chain. The question was whether it would *settle*. Could a daily snapshot of BOINC compute, agreed on by independent scraper operators, emerge reliably without a central coordinator? Could the magnitude payout system avoid being gamed by zero-credit beacons or hijacked project whitelists?

Most of 2015's work was answering those questions in code. Wallet releases through the year (3.5.x line) iterated on scraper consensus, beacon expiration semantics, and the rules around when a project could be added to or removed from the active whitelist. Each release was small; together they hardened the launch architecture into something that ran without daily intervention.

## What the year proved

Three things were established by year-end:

- **Daily superblocks worked.** {{stat:total_superblocks}} of them issued without persistent quorum failure.
- **Project whitelisting could be community-governed.** Inclusion rules and removal mechanics were exercised against real BOINC projects coming and going.
- **The beacon model scaled.** Researcher count grew without the registration system collapsing under it.

By year-end the chain had processed {{stat:total_txs}} transactions and {{stat:grc_moved}} GRC had moved between addresses — orders of magnitude more day-to-day exercise than year one. The experiment was functional infrastructure now, not a prototype.

## What stayed unsolved

The original magnitude formula treated BOINC credit as effectively linear: more compute = more reward. That left two open problems for the years ahead:

- A whale researcher with industrial-scale BOINC throughput captured outsized magnitude versus a hobbyist contributing meaningfully.
- The reward inflation curve was loosely tied to credit volume rather than network economics, with no built-in correction.

Both would shape the debates that produced the [Fern hard fork](/blocks/2018) three years later.
