## 4.0.0.0 — the constant-block-reward fork

Gridcoin's first major economic hard fork landed in 2018. The 4.0.0.0 mandatory release shipped on October 19; the hard fork activated at **block 1,420,000** on November 8, 2018. {{stat:total_blocks}} blocks were minted across the year, with the chain's reward economics switching mid-stride from one model to another.

The headline change: **interest-based block reward was replaced by a flat constant block reward**. Stakers used to earn an interest-like rate proportional to their stake size, on top of any research rewards. That coupling produced compounding effects whales could exploit and made the network's mint rate hard to predict. After the fork, every block mints a fixed amount regardless of staker balance — predictable per-block, predictable per-day, easy to reason about.

## What else 4.0.0.0 changed

It was a mandatory release, so it carried more than just the reward formula:

- **Coinstake output count raised to 8.** Linux nodes could now stake superblocks using forwarded contracts — the previous output limit had blocked them in some configurations.
- **Bitcoin hash implementation port.** The upstream Bitcoin codebase's hash routines replaced Gridcoin's older implementations, aligning the chain with mainline cryptographic practice.
- **Bug fixes around staking math, RPC, and rain operations.** Routine but mandatory, the kind of cleanup a hard fork enables in one go.

A 4.0.1 hotfix shipped soon after to resolve some bad-fork edge cases, and the 4.0.x line continued for the rest of the year stabilising the new economics.

## A clarification — 4.0.0.0 is NOT the "Fern" release

This is worth saying explicitly because the names get conflated: **Fern is 5.0.0.0 (2020)**, not 4.0.0.0. The 2018 hard fork is just "the 4.0 fork" — it changed reward economics but didn't touch the deeper architecture. The famous beacon-overhaul, team-requirement removal, codebase rewrite that people associate with "Fern" is two years later, at [block 2,053,000](/blocks/2020) under 5.0.0.0.

## What 2018 set up

By year-end the chain was operating under predictable mint economics for the first time. {{stat:total_superblocks}} superblocks were issued during the year, {{stat:total_txs}} transactions processed, and {{stat:grc_moved}} GRC moved. The constant-block-reward floor would remain the basis of subsequent reward design — every later upgrade (Fern, MRC, mandatory sidestaking) layers on top of the 4.0.0.0 foundation rather than replacing it.
