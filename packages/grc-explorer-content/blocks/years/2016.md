## A year without an upheaval

Some years on a chain are inflection points; 2016 wasn't one. {{stat:total_blocks}} blocks landed, {{stat:total_superblocks}} superblocks were issued, {{stat:total_txs}} transactions moved {{stat:grc_moved}} GRC, and the chain just kept going.

That's exactly what mattered. After [2015's shakeout](/blocks/2015), 2016 was the year proof-of-research stopped being a thesis and started being routine. Wallets ran, researchers added beacons, scrapers agreed, and a cryptocurrency was — in the most boring way possible — paying for science.

## What started cooking under the surface

The magnitude formula's open questions from 2015 didn't go away. A whale researcher with industrial-scale BOINC hardware still captured outsized rewards. The block-subsidy curve was still loosely tied to credit rather than economics. Forum debates around economic reform began in earnest this year — not yet committee-organised, just members of the community articulating problems and prototyping fixes.

None of it shipped in 2016. But every Fern-era proposal that landed three years later traces some thread to discussion this year.

## A year of researcher growth

Where 2016 *did* show change: the BOINC researcher pool. More projects were whitelisted and more individual researchers registered beacons. [Superblocks](/superblocks) for the year reflect a steadily-growing CPID set rather than a fixed early cohort.

The year ended with the chain on stable footing and a quiet build-up of community design work waiting to ship. That work would surface increasingly in 2017.
