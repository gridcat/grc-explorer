## There was a Gridcoin before this one

The chain you are browsing, Gridcoin **Research**, starts at [block 0 in October 2014](/block/0). It wasn't the first Gridcoin. On October 16, 2013, a pseudonymous developer who went by **Rob Halförd** launched a coin after announcing it on the CryptocurrencyTalk forum ([^Gridcoin Classic, gridcoin.us wiki](https://gridcoin.us/wiki/gridcoin-classic.html)). The official Bitcointalk thread followed on November 3, under the title "first coin utilizing BOINC." The pitch the project still uses fits the whole idea into five words: *why mine when you can research?*

That coin is now called Gridcoin Classic. It had a different chain, a different codebase and a different block history. This explorer doesn't index it and never will, because Classic isn't the chain it talks to. This page exists so the story starts where it actually starts.

"Rob Halförd" was not the founder's real name. The handle nods to Rob Halford, the Judas Priest singer. The founder stayed pseudonymous throughout, which mattered more than it sounds, as the treasury section below shows.

## What Classic actually ran

Classic was a **proof-of-work** coin, but an unusual one. Its white paper, published in May 2014, describes a hybrid: hashing across four algorithms (scrypt, skein, groestl and cubehash) secured part of the chain, and a "Proof of Boinc" share paid the rest ([^Gridcoin Classic white paper, gridcoin.us](https://gridcoin.us/assets/docs/classic/Gridcoin-White-Paper.pdf)). To earn that share you had to run [BOINC](https://boinc.berkeley.edu/), be a member of Team Gridcoin, and have your recent average credit check out against a third-party stats site. The client even capped its built-in miner at half the daily blocks, so the science side couldn't be crowded out.

The aim was right, but the implementation wasn't. The wiki's own summary is that the early client "directly measured BOINC CPU utility," and that was easy to fake ([^Proof-of-Research history, Gridcoin-Wiki archive](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Wiki/wiki/zArchive~Proof-of-Research)). You didn't have to do the science to look like you had. For a coin whose whole reason to exist was "the work is real," that was a reason to start over, not a bug to patch.

One piece did survive, for a while. The Team Gridcoin requirement carried over to Research and stayed until [Fern](/blocks/2020) removed it in 2020.

## The rebuild, decided in public

By February 2014 the community was talking about a redesign, and the name "Proof-of-Research" came out of those forum threads. The plan was to drop the gameable measurement, tie researcher identity to BOINC itself, and secure the chain by staking instead of hashing. Testnet trials ran from March to October 2014. Halförd explained where the idea came from in a May 2014 interview: watching bitcoin go from a cent to $500, the founder "could not believe how much heat was wasted collectively."

## How Classic ended

Classic wasn't switched off. It wound down on a schedule while holders moved across:

- 2014-10-12: the conversion window opens. Holders burn Classic coins to an address with no possible private key and receive Research coins at **1 Classic to 10 Research**.
- 2015-04-26: the window closes after about six months.

During the window the two coins traded under different tickers. Classic became **GRCX**, and Research took **GRC**. The number of confirmations required grew with the size of the burn, from 10 for small amounts to 300 for the largest. Classic itself ran out the clock at a fixed 10-coin block reward for five months, then 0.1.

## The treasury

The Research coins for the conversion had to come from somewhere. On launch day, [block 10](/block/10) minted 340,569,880 GRC in a single block: the projected Classic supply times ten. Conversions were paid out of it, and whatever nobody claimed became the foundation fund ([^Gridcoin foundation fund, gridcoin.us wiki](https://gridcoin.us/wiki/foundation.html)).

That fund sat in an address Halförd alone controlled. It paid for project costs such as developer pay, servers and marketing, and by mid-2017 it had shrunk to about 22 million GRC. It only moved into a 2-of-3 community multisig in August 2018, so for more than three years a single pseudonymous person held the project's treasury.

## Why it belongs in the history

If you came here for 2013 block data, the section below is empty, as it should be: Classic isn't on this chain. Everything that wasn't a block carried forward. The thesis that compute should pay for science, the BOINC approach, the founder, the early community and the lesson that the science has to be impossible to fake all moved to [the 2014 chain](/blocks/2014), which rebuilt the machine to deliver on the idea.
