## A consolidation year

[Kermit's Mom (2022)](/blocks/2022) had been a busy mandatory release — MRC introduction, CBR refinements, codebase modernisation. 2023 was the year all of that had to *just work*. {{stat:total_blocks}} blocks, {{stat:total_superblocks}} superblocks, {{stat:total_txs}} transactions, {{stat:grc_moved}} GRC moved — chain protocol unchanged from the prior year.

The 2023 wallet release line stayed in *leisure* (non-mandatory) territory:

- **5.4.5.0** (April 23, 2023) — wallet-stability bugfixes addressing edge cases around RPC, sync, and wallet GUI behaviour. No protocol change; safe to upgrade at the user's pace.

The 5.4.x line continued through smaller maintenance releases for the rest of the year.

## What MRC did to network behaviour in its first full year

A few patterns settled in over 2023:

- **Solo researchers used MRC.** The transaction-type became a regular sight in mempool traffic — not high volume, but present. Researchers without staking nodes were genuinely participating in the reward flow.
- **Stakers earned MRC fees.** Small but consistent additional revenue for wallets that stayed online.
- **Magnitude eligibility became less wallet-uptime-dependent.** A claim could be filed days or weeks after the magnitude was earned, smoothing out the participant pool.

None of this was newsworthy in the way a hard fork is, but it represented MRC's design working at scale rather than just at theory.

## Looking ahead

Behind the scenes, the next mandatory upgrade was being assembled. The themes:

- A **Treasury and Mandatory Sidestaking** poll passed by the community, establishing the framework for a protocol-enforced sidestake to the Gridcoin foundation (capped at 25% of any single coinstake's reward).
- A new **block version 14** was being designed to carry it.
- **HTLC support** (Hash Time-Locked Contracts) was on the roadmap — the building block atomic swaps would later use.

All of it was 2024-2026 work. 2023 was the calm before.
