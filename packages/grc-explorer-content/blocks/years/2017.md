## The year the data starts behaving

Look at the superblock column in the [history index](/history). It is zero for 2014, 2015 and 2016, and then 2017 it is not. That single discontinuity is the most important thing about this year, and there is a real story behind it.

The chain itself looked routine. **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks**, **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**, **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC** moved, **{{stat:grc_minted}} GRC** minted. The drama was in whether researchers actually got paid.

## The 200-hour superblock

For three years the daily magnitude snapshot had been unreliable, and in June 2017 it stopped working entirely. The network went somewhere north of 200 hours, more than a week, without a valid superblock. Researchers simply did not get paid for that whole stretch. The cause was a pile-up: a wave of new low-balance Windows wallets joined, CPIDs were changing faster than the scrapers could agree on a snapshot, and no node with enough stake weight could push a valid superblock through. It was finally rescued when a single large account had the weight to force one in.

That is the kind of failure that either kills a small project or fixes it. Gridcoin fixed it.

## V8, and the floor under the chain

The repository's kept changelog starts here, with **3.6.0.1 on August 22, 2017**, and it is not a coincidence that the recorded history of the codebase begins at the moment it got serious ([^Gridcoin Research CHANGELOG — github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/blob/master/CHANGELOG.md)). Two consensus changes anchor the year:

- [Block 1,010,000](/block/1010000) on **September 1, 2017**: the **V8 stake engine** activates. New kernel rules, security fixes, and the formalised superblock generation, scraper consensus, and beacon management that finally made a daily snapshot something the network produced on its own rather than something it occasionally managed.
- [Block 1,034,700](/block/1034700) on **September 28, 2017**: the `nGrandfather` cutover. Below this height the wallet skips most claim and superblock validation, treating the early chain as grandfathered-in. Above it, full validation is enforced. If you ever wonder why an explorer treats pre-2017 blocks differently, this is the line.

After this, superblocks land. Not perfectly, the 2017 count is partial because the fix arrived mid-year, but the system works. By 2019 it is effectively one a day, every day.

## The other 2017 story

Two things ran alongside the engineering. The crypto market went vertical, and Gridcoin went with it, from under a cent at the start of the year toward fifteen cents by year-end, with the actual peak still ahead in the first days of [2018](/blocks/2018). And in August the Ruhr-University Bochum researchers who had quietly reported flaws back in 2016 ran out of patience. A partial patch had shipped in 3.5.8.7 in March and introduced new problems; follow-up emails went unanswered. So in August 2017 they presented "Breaking and Fixing Gridcoin" at USENIX WOOT'17 and demonstrated the attacks live ([^Gridcoin — Wikipedia, citing USENIX WOOT'17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gridcoin)). Getting publicly broken on a conference stage during the biggest bull run of the project's life is not a good week, but the beacon hardening it forced is part of why the system later became trustworthy.

## What 2017 set up

Underneath all of it, the community was writing the economic reform it had been arguing about since 2016: detach the staker reward from an interest rate and pin it to a flat per-block subsidy. None of that shipped in 2017. It shipped a year later, at a block height worth remembering, in [2018](/blocks/2018).
