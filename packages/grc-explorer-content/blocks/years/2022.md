## The year solo crunchers got paid properly

The mandatory 5.4.0.0 release, codenamed **"Kermit's Mom,"** came out on August 1, 2022, and its v12 hard fork activated at [block 2,671,700](/block/2671700) at 18:47 UTC on August 28 ([^5.4.0.0 release notes, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/releases/tag/5.4.0.0)). The announcement had said "on or about August 31," so the block came a few days early. This archive goes by the block's own timestamp.

The headline feature is **Manual Reward Claims (MRC)**. If you ran BOINC on one machine and didn't keep a wallet online staking, the magnitude you built up was hard to cash in. The release notes put the effective minimum for solo crunching at 5,000 GRC or more, because without that much stake you'd wait a very long time for a block. After Kermit's Mom, any researcher with an active beacon can file a claim transaction for their own accrued reward and have a staker include it ([^Kermit's Mom announcement, jamescowens, hive.blog](https://hive.blog/gridcoin/@jamescowens/gridcoin-5-4-0-0-mandatory-kermit-s-mom-release)).

The rules often get summarised loosely, so here they are in full:

- There's no minimum claim amount, only a minimum time. A claim filed less than 14 days after your last reward forfeits all of it.
- At exactly 14 days the fee is 40% of the claim. The fee stays the same in absolute terms after that, so the longer you wait, the smaller it is as a share.
- Of the fee, 80% goes to the foundation and 20% to the staker who includes the claim. The split came from an on-chain poll in February to April 2022 and is written into the code. The release notes give a worked example: a 35 GRC claim at 14 days pays 21 GRC to the researcher, 11.2 to the foundation and 2.8 to the staker.
- A block can carry up to ten claims.

That changed who Gridcoin is for. A laptop running BOINC overnight could now earn GRC directly, and stakers picked up a small fee stream for including claims.

MRC took a long time. It was first proposed in the project's task repo on April 12, 2018 ([^Manual Reward Claim proposal, Gridcoin-Tasks #218](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Tasks/issues/218)), and it won the [2018 roadmap poll](/blocks/2018) on how to pay researchers. The code went in through 2022, and the issue was closed in May, four years after it opened.

## What else came with v12

A version gate is never just one feature. v12 also:

- tightened block timestamps, so a block can't be more than 128 seconds behind the previous one or ahead of network time;
- brought the v3 poll format, with poll types, minimum durations and participation thresholds enforced by consensus instead of by convention;
- required every whitelisted project to be re-registered with a GDPR-controls flag, which all fourteen were on the day of the fork;
- replaced OpenSSL with libsecp256k1 for signatures and added countermeasures against eclipse attacks.

## The rest of the release line

**5.3.3.0 "Janice"** on February 27 was a leisure release: automated release builds, a `-reindex` option, a sync progress indicator and a GUI privacy mode that hides balances. Its notes closed by promising that the next milestone would bring "the long awaited MRC." 5.4.1.0 in November added the `getmrcinfo` RPC and a diagnostics tool for the wallet.

The codenames are alphabetical, and by now the Muppets connection is hard to miss. The "J" was Janice, the guitarist in the Electric Mayhem band, and the "K" was Kermit's Mom, which made a consensus-critical hard fork share a name with Kermit the Frog's mother. The next milestone was already open, and its name was LaVerne.

## The whitelist

World Community Grid dropped off the whitelist in February while it moved from IBM to the Krembil Research Institute, and came back in September. GPUGrid was added in March and Asteroids@home in November. Collatz Conjecture and Cosmology@Home were removed. In November a poll opened on adding Folding@home through an adapter, which would make it the first project on the list that isn't on BOINC at all. It closed [in January](/blocks/2023).
