## The boring year on purpose

2019 is the first full calendar year the chain ran under the [constant block reward](/blocks/2018), and there is nothing in the consensus log to point at. No version gate, no landmark, no fork. **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks**, **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**, **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC** moved, **{{stat:grc_minted}} GRC** minted, and a superblock count that finally sits right around one per day for the whole year.

That last detail is the quiet payoff of everything that went wrong in 2017. The thing that used to fail for 200 hours at a stretch now happens every day without anyone watching it. Stakers staked, scrapers agreed, researchers ran BOINC, payouts went out. The 2018 fork was supposed to make the chain predictable, and 2019 is the year the chain demonstrated it by being uneventful. For a network this small, an uneventful year is not nothing. It is the product working.

## The maintenance line

The 4.0.x releases through the year were leisure, not mandatory, so they changed nothing about consensus:

- **4.0.3.0** (May 10) is the interesting one historically. It finished replacing the old "neural network" with a proper C++ scraper. The thing that caused the 2017 superblock crisis was being rewritten out of existence rather than patched around.
- **4.0.5.0 "Elizabeth"** (August 20) and **4.0.6.0 "Ernestine"** (October 22) were stability and wallet work.

If the codenames look like a slow march through a list of women's first names, that is because they are ([^Gridcoin Research releases — github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Research/releases)). Gridcoin gives nearly every release a personal codename, and the running joke gets cheekier later. It is a small thing, but it tells you the project is run by people who are enjoying it, which for a volunteer chain in a bear market is its own kind of resilience.

## What was actually happening

The real 2019 work was not in any shipped release. The development effort had moved to a near-complete rewrite of the Gridcoin-specific codebase, the thing that would land in 2020 as Fern: on the order of 240 pull requests and about ten months of work, with much of the heavy lifting credited to a contributor working under the handle cyrossignol ([^Fern release announcement — jamescowens, hive.blog](https://hive.blog/gridcoin/@jamescowens/gridcoin-5-0-0-0-mandatory-fern-release)). The hard questions deferred during the 2018 fork were getting answered in unmerged branches: deterministic superblock construction, removing the BOINC team requirement without breaking attribution, proving CPID ownership at beacon registration, computing rewards from the actual superblocks between stakes instead of a rolling average.

None of it touched mainnet in 2019. The protocol behaved exactly as it had since the [V10 fork](/blocks/2018). The year's significance is entirely that it bought the time to build [Fern](/blocks/2020) properly instead of in a hurry.
