## The chain you are actually looking at

[Block 0](/block/0) landed on **October 11, 2014, at 13:22 UTC**. That timestamp is the real beginning of this explorer's history, and everything in the archive sidebar counts forward from it.

Gridcoin Research was Rob Halförd's answer to a question Bitcoin never had a good response to: the network was spending the power budget of a small country on hash functions whose only output was more hash functions. What if the same incentive paid for protein folding, climate models, or cancer screening instead? [Gridcoin Classic](/blocks/2013) had asked that in 2013 and answered it badly. Research was the rebuild.

## How it actually started

The chain did not begin under proof-of-research. The first roughly 2,000 blocks were plain scrypt proof-of-work, mined deliberately to bootstrap a safe genesis before anything fancier switched on ([^Proof-of-Research history — Gridcoin-Wiki archive, github.com/gridcoin-community](https://github.com/gridcoin-community/Gridcoin-Wiki/wiki/zArchive~Proof-of-Research)). You can watch the handover happen: by [block 2050](/block/2050), two days after genesis, the chain is already minting proof-of-stake blocks. After that the proof-of-work era was over almost as soon as it started, and the chain ran on staking with a research-reward layer on top, a proof-of-stake design descended from Peercoin by way of Novacoin and Blackcoin.

Two ideas made it interesting from the first week:

- **Beacons.** The unit of identity is not a mining rig, it is a signed message tying a Gridcoin address to a BOINC CPID. Register once, keep contributing, keep getting paid. No hardware arms race.
- **The magnitude idea.** Roughly once a day the network agrees on a snapshot of every whitelisted BOINC project and how much each researcher contributed, and rewards flow from that. Early whitelisted projects were the BOINC mainstays of the era: World Community Grid, Rosetta@home, POEM@home, the SETI@home generation.

There was no premie, the block target settled to 90 seconds (a change the chain made at [block 85400](/block/85400) in December 2014), and the coin's whole pitch was that the electricity did something a scientist could use.

## What the numbers say, and what they don't

2014 was a partial year, October to December, so the chain recorded **{{stat:total_blocks}} blocks** and **{{stat:total_txs}} transactions**. Two figures jump out and both have the same explanation.

**{{stat:grc_minted}} GRC minted** in three months, far more than any later full year in this archive, and **{{stat:grc_moved}} GRC moved**, an order of magnitude above anything that follows. That is not organic chain traffic. It is the [Classic-to-Research conversion](/blocks/2013) running in real time: a one-way burn-and-mint at a 1-to-10 ratio, plus the era's high block subsidy, plus the churn of an entire coin's holders migrating across in a few months. The chain was being populated, not used.

One thing you will *not* see is superblocks. This explorer indexes **zero** for 2014, and that is historically correct rather than a gap in the data. The daily magnitude snapshot existed as an idea, but the machinery that makes it land reliably on-chain did not come together until 2017. The early chain paid research rewards through a much rougher process, and "a clean superblock every day" was an aspiration, not a fact. The story of how that got fixed is [2017's](/blocks/2017), and it is not a tidy one.

## What 2014 set up

The foundation was in place by New Year: proof-of-stake security, beacons, the magnitude concept, a working BOINC bridge. The open questions were the ones the rest of this archive answers. Could a daily snapshot be agreed without a trusted operator? Could the reward economics survive contact with whales and a moving BOINC project list? Each became the defining fight of a later year. They all start here.
